Compounding and time value of money: how to build your first financial system from a balance sheet?

30-second key takeaways (Key Takeaways)

  • The core of compounding is “time” and “reinvestment”, not short‑term windfall profits.
  • The first step in building a financial system is to quantify risk by capturing net worth through an asset‑liability statement.
  • An emergency fund should cover at least six months of fixed expenses to withstand market volatility.
  • Inflation is a silent killer; long‑term asset allocation must include productive assets that can hedge inflation.

1. Deep breakdown of the compound interest formula (複利公式)

Einstein called compound interest 'the eighth wonder of the world.' Its mathematical expression is:FV = PV * (1 + r)^nIn this formula we see the three variables that determine final wealth (FV): initial principal (PV), rate of return (r), and time (n).

Most people overfocus on return (r) and ignore the exponential power of time (n). Even with slightly lower returns, a long enough time horizon can produce astonishing final outcomes.

2. Inflation defense and asset value

Inflation erodes the purchasing power of money. If your financial system contains only cash, your real wealth is shrinking every year. To defend against inflation, the system must shift toward "assets", especially those that generate cash flow.

Recommended reading: Ray Dalio's Principles，learn how to build decision systems using objective logic.

3. Practical personal balance sheet (assets & liabilities)

The cornerstone of a personal finance system is “transparency.” You must manage your finances like a company. Building a Personal Balance Sheet lets you clearly see your Assets (Assets) and Liabilities (Liabilities).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does compounding require adding principal every year?
Not necessarily, but continuing a regular DCA habit produces a "double acceleration" effect. A lump sum benefits from compound growth; disciplined monthly or annual additional contributions expand the capital base and make the compound curve climb more steeply.
What to do if market inflation is higher than investment returns?
If nominal returns are below inflation, real purchasing power shrinks. That’s why bank time deposits alone cannot beat inflation; asset allocation should include assets with pricing power and productivity (e.g., broad equity index ETFs, inflation‑protected bonds or high‑quality real estate) to achieve real positive returns above inflation. ETF
Where should an emergency fund be held?
The core of an emergency fund is high liquidity and capital preservation. Recommended vehicles are high-yield checking/savings, digital banks’ high-yield savings or ultra-short-term T-bills. Do not place emergency funds in equities or highly volatile products to avoid forced realizations at losses during market crashes.

Advantages and target audience

Suitable for beginners in personal finance, emphasizing stability and long‑term planning. Helps reduce financial anxiety and build a predictable path of wealth growth.

Challenges and cautions

Requires great patience and discipline. In the early years (first 5–10 years) the compounding effect is not obvious, making it easy to give up or turn to high‑risk speculation out of impatience.
