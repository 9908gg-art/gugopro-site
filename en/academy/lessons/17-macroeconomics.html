macro observation

Macro data are not for predicting every market inflection point but to help you understand “which variables are changing discount rates, cash flows and risk premia.”

1. Break macro into three transmission chains

First is growth: demand, employment and corporate revenues affect cash flow. Second is inflation: wages and goods and service prices affect real purchasing power. Third are financial conditions: policy rates, credit spreads and liquidity affect valuation multiples. The three need not move together, so do not rely on a single indicator.

Asset price ≈ future cash flows ÷ discount rate

2. Separate nominal and real

Nominal returns do not subtract inflation; real returns better reflect changes in purchasing power. When inflation rises without a matching rise in nominal rates, real rates fall and may stimulate demand; conversely, tighter real financial conditions can depress valuations and investment.

3. Build a monthly observation checklist

  1. Record inflation trends and the market’s deviation from inflation expectations.
  2. Watch policy rates and credit spreads, not just headlines.
  3. Translate macro assumptions into portfolio constraints, e.g., reduce duration or preserve liquidity.
  4. Use scenarios instead of assertions: what would a soft landing, recession or re‑inflation each affect?
